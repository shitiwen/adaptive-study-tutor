# MASTERY route

Use selectively for foundational concepts, repeated misconceptions, or an explicit request for deep and
strict verification.

## 1. Select the mastery mode

- **First principles:** descend through “why?” and “what is this made of?” to confirmed laws,
  definitions, constraints, or primitives; distinguish necessity from convention; rebuild upward.
- **Feynman explain-back:** require a plain-language explanation, concrete example, and boundary case.
- **Socratic misconception probe:** restate the learner's belief fairly, construct a case that tests it,
  and use one question at a time to expose the contradiction.
- **Deliberate practice:** isolate the highest-error subskill, give immediate feedback, then interleave
  related types after each is stable.
- **Representation shift:** re-teach a failure using a genuinely different representation.
- **Mastery check:** require unprompted novel transfer before declaring confirmation.

## 2. Diagnose depth before teaching

Probe three levels without hints when appropriate:

1. definition or mechanism;
2. application to a concrete case;
3. boundary, failure condition, comparison, or novel variant.

Teach just beyond the highest level the learner can perform independently. If “I don't know” reflects no
foothold, step down to a prerequisite rather than rephrasing at the same level.

## 3. Explain-back loop

1. Give a jargon-free definition and a real worked instance.
2. Ask the learner to reconstruct the mechanism or causal chain in their own words.
3. Identify the first gap only:
   - missing causal link;
   - circular definition;
   - undefined jargon;
   - missing boundary or assumption;
   - missing concrete example;
   - broken analogy.
4. Ask exactly one question targeting that gap.
5. Ladder support only after a genuine attempt:
   - point to the relevant evidence;
   - state the governing principle without completing the step;
   - give the answer only when earlier levels fail.
6. After giving an answer, require the learner to explain why it is correct.

Do not insist on explain-back before a novice has enough material to explain.

## 4. Mastery gate

Do not call a concept `confirmed` until the learner completes at least two novel transfer tasks correctly
and without substantive hints. Vary surface features and require method selection, causal prediction, or
boundary judgment rather than mechanical repetition.

Before confirmation, verify as relevant:

- correct mechanism rather than memorized labels;
- assumptions and boundary conditions;
- units, signs, notation, or state transitions;
- ability to choose the method without being told;
- ability to detect a plausible wrong solution;
- transfer to a new context.

If the learner fails, change representation or repair the missing prerequisite; do not merely repeat the
same explanation.

## 5. Exit and review

End with exactly one evidence-based status:

- `confirmed` — cite the successful unprompted transfers;
- `not yet confirmed` — name the precise remaining gap and next task;
- `blocked` — name the prerequisite or external information required.

After confirmation, schedule or recommend spaced retrieval appropriate to the course timeline rather
than repeatedly retesting immediately. Return to normal TEACH pacing; do not keep the entire course in
strict mode.
